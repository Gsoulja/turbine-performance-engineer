Can an AI understand a machine it has never seen? July 9th, 2023. A wind farm in Scotland. The wind is blowing. One turbine makes 8 megawatt-hours. Another, on the same farm, makes nothing. All day. Our AI had never seen this brand of turbine. It still caught it. We're sololeveling, and we're building a performance engineer for every wind turbine.

European wind farms produce about 9% less energy than they were financed on. Every 1% recovered is worth about £3,700 per turbine a year. But finding out why still depends on each maker's alarm codes, and on the vendor's word.

What we built

From raw signals to a checked answer

Open data, labels checked against independent sources: raw status logs, grid dispatch records, a second wind sensor, operator comments.3
We built it on open data: three UK wind farms, 41 turbines, two makers, all in TimeNet, every label checked against independent evidence. OpenTSLM reads six raw signals through a fusion adapter we added, and answers in words.

The headline

Trained on Senvion. Tested on Siemens.

Hill of Towie (Siemens): 2,556 stops over six years and 1,741 stop causes over nine, each scored once. At home, the tree is as good or better.4
Trained only on Senvion and tested once on Siemens, it caught 92% of stops, against 73% for a gradient-boosting tree. On the cause of a stop, its F1 score was 84%, against the tree's 77%.

Demo

The whole loop, in one tool

The monitor replays real data: Hill of Towie (Siemens, never seen in training) and Kelmarsh (Senvion). The Copilot drafts; a person approves.10
Here's the whole loop, live, on real data. Detect: turbine 12 shows stopped. Explain: what it cost, by cause, each answer next to its independent checks. Act: Fix first ranks turbines by money at stake, and the copilot drafts the work order for a person to approve. Verify: turbine 13's new blades. Learn: the engineer marks the answer. It stays pending until independent evidence agrees, and a retrained model replaces this one only if it beats the public tests.

Appendix

The limits we publish with the results

QuestionWhat still holds it back
StopsGradient boosting ranks stop days slightly better (average precision 99.6% vs 98.9%); our edge is a threshold that carries over to another maker. Stops under 60 minutes are not in the test.
Curtailment8.1% of clean days alarmed on Hill of Towie 2025–26. On later Senvion days 43% caught: most logged days show no drop in power. Our curtailment language model stays below the tree.
CauseAt home the tree answers: our model failed the 2016–18 Senvion test, mostly where pitch was not recorded. Its Hill of Towie win is one farm, labelled by undocumented stop timers, with no maintenance class.
